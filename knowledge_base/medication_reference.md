# Medication Reference Guide — Riverside Medical Center

## Antihypertensives

**Common medications:** Lisinopril, Amlodipine, Losartan, Metoprolol, Hydrochlorothiazide

**Key patient instructions:**
- Take at the same time each day. Do not stop abruptly without consulting your provider.
- ACE inhibitors (Lisinopril): may cause a dry cough. Report any swelling of face, lips, or tongue immediately.
- Beta-blockers (Metoprolol): may cause fatigue or dizziness. Avoid sudden position changes.
- Diuretics (Hydrochlorothiazide): increase urination. Stay hydrated and monitor potassium levels.

**Common interactions:** NSAIDs can reduce effectiveness. Potassium supplements with ACE inhibitors may cause hyperkalemia. Avoid grapefruit with calcium channel blockers (Amlodipine).

## Diabetes Medications

**Common medications:** Metformin, Glipizide, Insulin (various types), Jardiance, Ozempic

**Key patient instructions:**
- Metformin: take with food to reduce GI side effects. Report any unusual muscle pain.
- Sulfonylureas (Glipizide): can cause low blood sugar. Always carry a glucose source.
- Insulin: rotate injection sites. Store unopened vials in refrigerator. Monitor blood sugar regularly.
- GLP-1 agonists (Ozempic): inject once weekly. Nausea is common initially and usually improves.

**Common interactions:** Metformin — hold before contrast dye procedures. Alcohol can increase hypoglycemia risk with sulfonylureas. Steroids can raise blood sugar significantly.

## Antihistamines

**Common medications:** Cetirizine (Zyrtec), Loratadine (Claritin), Diphenhydramine (Benadryl), Fexofenadine (Allegra)

**Key patient instructions:**
- Second-generation (Cetirizine, Loratadine, Fexofenadine) cause less drowsiness — preferred for daily use.
- First-generation (Diphenhydramine) causes significant drowsiness. Do not drive or operate machinery.
- Take consistently during allergy season for best results.

**Common interactions:** Diphenhydramine adds to sedation from alcohol, opioids, or sleep aids. Avoid combining multiple antihistamines.

## NSAIDs (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs)

**Common medications:** Ibuprofen (Advil/Motrin), Naproxen (Aleve), Aspirin, Celecoxib (Celebrex)

**Key patient instructions:**
- Take with food to reduce stomach irritation.
- Do not exceed recommended doses. Long-term use increases GI and cardiovascular risk.
- Avoid if you have a history of stomach ulcers or kidney disease.
- Aspirin: do not stop if prescribed for heart protection without consulting your provider.

**Common interactions:** NSAIDs reduce effectiveness of antihypertensives and increase bleeding risk with blood thinners (warfarin, apixaban). Avoid combining multiple NSAIDs. Use caution with SSRIs due to increased bleeding risk.

## General Guidance

- Always verify patient allergies before discussing medications.
- Refer to the prescribing provider for dose changes or new prescriptions.
- Encourage patients to use a single pharmacy for interaction checking.
- Report any suspected adverse reactions to the care team.
