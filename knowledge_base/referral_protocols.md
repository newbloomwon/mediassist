# Referral Protocols — Riverside Medical Center

## Cardiology

**Refer when:**
- New onset chest pain not explained by musculoskeletal or GI causes
- Abnormal ECG or cardiac biomarkers
- Uncontrolled hypertension despite two or more medications
- Heart failure symptoms (edema, dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea)
- Palpitations with syncope or near-syncope

**Include in referral:** Current medications, recent vitals (especially BP trends), ECG results if available, symptom timeline.

## Endocrinology

**Refer when:**
- Type 2 diabetes with A1C above 9% despite maximum oral therapy
- Suspected Type 1 diabetes or LADA
- Thyroid nodules or abnormal thyroid function not responding to treatment
- Adrenal or pituitary disorders suspected
- Recurrent hypoglycemia without clear cause

**Include in referral:** Recent A1C, fasting glucose trends, current diabetes regimen, thyroid labs if applicable.

## Neurology

**Refer when:**
- New seizure activity
- Chronic headaches not responding to standard treatment
- Suspected multiple sclerosis or neuropathy symptoms
- Tremor, movement disorders, or cognitive decline
- TIA or stroke history requiring specialist follow-up

**Include in referral:** Symptom description and timeline, current neurological medications, imaging results if available, cognitive screening scores.

## Orthopedics

**Refer when:**
- Joint pain with functional limitation not responding to conservative treatment (6+ weeks)
- Suspected fracture or ligament tear
- Chronic back pain with neurological symptoms (numbness, weakness)
- Post-surgical orthopedic follow-up needed

**Include in referral:** Affected joint/area, duration, treatments tried, imaging results, functional limitations.

## General Referral Guidelines

- Obtain patient consent before sending the referral.
- Include the patient's current medication list and allergy information.
- Specify urgency: routine (2-4 weeks), urgent (within 1 week), or emergent (same day).
- Document the referral in the patient's medical record.
