# Symptom Triage Guidelines — Riverside Medical Center

## Chest Pain

**Urgency: HIGH — Potential Emergency**

Ask the patient:
- When did the pain start? Is it constant or intermittent?
- Can you describe the pain (sharp, dull, pressure, burning)?
- Does it radiate to the arm, jaw, neck, or back?
- Are you experiencing shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, or dizziness?
- Do you have a history of heart disease or high blood pressure?

**Triage decision:**
- If the patient reports sudden onset, radiating pain, shortness of breath, or sweating: **recommend calling 911 or going to the ER immediately**. Do not delay.
- If the pain is mild, localized, and associated with movement or breathing: recommend an urgent care visit within 24 hours.
- If the patient has a known history of GERD or musculoskeletal chest pain with similar prior episodes: recommend a routine appointment.

## Shortness of Breath

**Urgency: HIGH to MODERATE**

Ask the patient:
- Did this come on suddenly or gradually?
- Are you experiencing chest pain, wheezing, or coughing?
- Do you have a history of asthma, COPD, or heart failure?
- Can you speak in full sentences?
- Have you had recent surgery, travel, or prolonged immobility?

**Triage decision:**
- Sudden onset with chest pain, inability to speak in full sentences, or recent immobility: **ER immediately** (possible PE or cardiac event).
- Gradual onset with known asthma/COPD and wheezing: urgent care or same-day appointment.
- Mild breathlessness with exertion only, no other symptoms: routine appointment.

## Headache

**Urgency: MODERATE to LOW**

Ask the patient:
- How severe is the pain on a scale of 1-10?
- When did it start? Is this a new type of headache for you?
- Are you experiencing visual changes, neck stiffness, fever, or confusion?
- Do you have a history of migraines?
- Have you had any recent head trauma?

**Triage decision:**
- "Worst headache of my life," sudden onset, neck stiffness, fever, or confusion: **ER immediately** (possible meningitis or subarachnoid hemorrhage).
- Severe migraine with known history, not responding to usual treatment: urgent care.
- Mild to moderate headache, consistent with tension or known migraine pattern: routine appointment or self-care guidance.

## Abdominal Pain

**Urgency: MODERATE to HIGH**

Ask the patient:
- Where exactly is the pain? Upper, lower, left, right?
- Is it sharp or cramping? Constant or comes and goes?
- Are you experiencing nausea, vomiting, fever, or changes in bowel habits?
- When did you last eat? Any recent dietary changes?
- Is there any possibility of pregnancy?

**Triage decision:**
- Severe right lower quadrant pain with fever: **ER** (possible appendicitis).
- Severe upper abdominal pain radiating to back with nausea: **ER** (possible pancreatitis or gallbladder).
- Mild cramping with dietary changes, no fever: routine appointment.

## Fever

**Urgency: MODERATE**

Ask the patient:
- What is your temperature? How long have you had it?
- Are you experiencing any other symptoms (cough, pain, rash)?
- Have you traveled recently or been exposed to illness?
- Are you immunocompromised or on immunosuppressive medications?

**Triage decision:**
- Temperature over 103F (39.4C) with confusion, stiff neck, or rash: **ER**.
- Temperature over 101F (38.3C) for more than 3 days: urgent care or same-day appointment.
- Low-grade fever with mild cold symptoms: self-care with follow-up if not improving in 48 hours.

## Dizziness

**Urgency: LOW to MODERATE**

Ask the patient:
- Is the room spinning (vertigo) or do you feel lightheaded/faint?
- When does it occur — standing up, turning your head, all the time?
- Are you experiencing hearing loss, ringing in ears, or nausea?
- Are you taking any new medications?
- Do you have a history of blood pressure issues?

**Triage decision:**
- Dizziness with slurred speech, weakness on one side, or severe headache: **call 911** (possible stroke).
- Vertigo with hearing changes: urgent care or same-day appointment.
- Lightheadedness when standing, possibly medication-related: routine appointment.
